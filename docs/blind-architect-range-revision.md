## Biological scope

Bat ranges depend on species, call level and frequency, reflecting target and environment. Surlykke and Kalko's field measurements and sonar estimates produced a maximum estimated prey range of 10–17 m for the longest-ranging species in their sample: [Echolocating Bats Cry Out Loud to Detect Their Prey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2323577/). This does not establish a universal indoor human-detection distance. A 10–20-block exposed-body target is an inference for a fictional creature.

Passive listening is distinct from active echo. Captive pallid bats localized brief cricket impact sounds at up to 4.9 m: [Fuzessery et al., 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8441123/). That supports the sensory distinction, not the exact chosen breath range. A [CSIR-CSIO sensor specification](https://www.csio.res.in/upload/PDF/motiondet.pdf) lists human ground-motion detection up to 30 m; instrumentation performance is not proof of animal sensitivity or propagation through arbitrary cave rock. The structural coefficients and range remain authored and uncalibrated.

Darkness itself does not attenuate acoustics. Static cover and clutter affect the model. Indirect echoes, detailed reverberation, ambient-noise masking and elastic wave modes are not implemented by this revision.

## Possible next realism work — proposals only

A useful next step is active checking of uncertain echoes: change head angle, take a small step to another listening position, and reduce uncertainty only if fresh physical returns agree. Existing head scanning and static clutter handling provide a starting point. Bats alter call timing and head movements in clutter, though individuals use different strategies: [Mao et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27914429/). This proposed movement policy is an inference for the creature, not a measured bat algorithm.

Received background noise could mask quiet cues in the same acoustic channel, with weaker masking for louder nearby signals and more active checking under uncertainty. Machinery or flowing water should contribute only after real propagation reaches the receiver; audible noise need not share the echo band. Avoid treating a noisy machine as guaranteed invisibility: [Foskolos et al., 2022](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/225/3/jeb242957/274313/Echolocating-Daubenton-s-bats-are-resilient-to) observed successful target approaches under strong masking, along with call-level adjustments and longer completion times.

A later bounded one-bounce echo model could let exposed rock around an open corner return a weak, ambiguous path. [Allen and Berkley's room-acoustics model](https://jontalle.web.engr.illinois.edu/Public/AllenBerkley79.pdf) is a primary reference for geometric reflections; adapting them to this cave remains proposed work. Delay, reflection angle, material loss and both outgoing/returning geometry must be checked. An indirect return should identify a broad candidate region; it must not reveal an occluded player's true coordinate. A sealed partition remains a negative control. Detailed wave simulation, new ranges and additional automatic views are not part of the current removal.
